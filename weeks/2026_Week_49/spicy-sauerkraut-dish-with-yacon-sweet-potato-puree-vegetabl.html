Chef's notes & cookware

  • Use the big pot for boiling the potatoes, the medium pot for preparing the sauerkraut mixture, replacing the 'saucepan', and the frying pan for crisping the vegetable bacon bits (steps 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8).
  • Before heating, chop all potatoes, finely chop the shallot, rinse and squeeze the sauerkraut, and dice the yacon. Have all spices and liquids measured out for smooth transitions (steps 2, 3, 4, 6).
  • Maximize efficiency by starting the potatoes in the big pot and immediately begin sautéing the shallot for the sauerkraut in the medium pot. This allows both main components to cook concurrently (steps 2, 3).
  • Once potatoes are tender, drain them quickly, reserving some liquid, and mash immediately in the big pot with butter and reserved liquid. Do not let them cool down, as this makes mashing more difficult (step 8).
  • Adjust the sourness of your sauerkraut by rinsing it more or less in step 4; a lighter rinse will yield a tangier result, while a thorough rinse mellows the flavour (step 4).
  • Don't forget to use some of the reserved potato cooking liquid to achieve the perfect consistency for both the mashed potatoes and to moisten the sauerkraut mixture, as noted in Step 8 (step 8).
